Spatial Patterns

Budapest Metropolitan Area

NO₂ Change: -3.5 µg/m³

Land Cover: Stable urban, minor expansion

Significant air quality improvement despite urban growth, likely due to emission regulations and traffic management.

Great Hungarian Plain

NO₂ Change: -1.6 µg/m³

Land Cover: Stable agriculture

Agricultural regions show moderate NO₂ reduction, correlated with seasonal patterns and farming practices.

Northern Highlands

NO₂ Change: -1.2 µg/m³

Land Cover: Forest gain (+2.1%)

Low baseline NO₂ levels with slight improvement. Increased forest cover contributes to air quality.

Conclusions

  • ✓ Overall air quality improvement: NO₂ concentrations decreased by an average of 2.3 µg/m³ across Hungary between 2021 and 2023
  • ✓ Urban areas show the most significant reduction (-3.1 µg/m³), indicating effectiveness of emission control policies
  • ✓ Land cover remained relatively stable, with minor forest gain (85 km²) and urban expansion (320 km²)
  • ✓ Strong spatial correlation between land use type and NO₂ change patterns
  • ✓ Agricultural zones exhibit seasonal variability in NO₂ levels linked to farming activities
